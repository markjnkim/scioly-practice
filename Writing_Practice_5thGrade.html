💡 Before You Write: Planning

  1. Pick your side clearly. Don't try to argue both sides. Choose one position and commit to it — even if you secretly see both sides in real life.
  2. Brainstorm 3 reasons. Write down 3 reasons you support your position. Then pick the 2 strongest for your essay (or all 3 if time allows).
  3. Think of a specific example for each reason. "Studies show..." or "For example, when I..." are great ways to add evidence.
  4. Write a quick outline before starting: Intro → Reason 1 + detail → Reason 2 + detail → Conclusion. Even 2 minutes of planning saves 10 minutes of confusion.

✏️ While You Write

  1. Start strong. Your first sentence should grab attention. Try a question ("Have you ever stayed up until midnight doing homework?") or a bold statement.
  2. State your opinion in sentence 2 or 3. Don't make the reader guess what side you're on. Be direct: "I strongly believe that students should not have homework every night because..."
  3. Use transition words between paragraphs: "First," "Another important reason," "Additionally," "Most importantly," "In conclusion."
  4. End with power. Your last sentence should leave the reader thinking. Restate your opinion in a new way — don't just copy your introduction.

🔍 After You Write: Revision Checklist

✅Read it aloud. Your ear catches mistakes your eyes miss. If you stumble reading it, fix that sentence.
✅Check your opening. Does your first paragraph clearly state your opinion? If not, add or strengthen that sentence.
✅Check your ending. Does your conclusion do more than repeat your intro? Add a "so what?" — why does this matter?
✅Hunt for boring words. Circle every "good," "bad," "nice," "thing," and "stuff." Replace each one with a more specific word.
✅Sentence variety check. Circle the first word of every sentence. If more than 3 in a row start the same way, change some up.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

MistakeFix
Writing "I think that I think that..."State your opinion once, clearly, then move on to reasons
Starting every sentence with "I"Try "Studies show...", "Many students...", "One example is..."
Saying "because it's fun/boring/good/bad"Give a SPECIFIC reason: "because homework takes time away from sports, family, and sleep"
Forgetting a conclusionAlways end with a paragraph that wraps up — even 2–3 sentences
Run-on sentencesIf a sentence has 3 "ands," break it into two sentences
